Introduction to semantic modelling

A concept scheme (in SKOS: ConceptScheme) is a structured collection of concepts that carry a fixed, shared meaning within a particular domain. The goal is for everyone working with the model — people and systems alike — to use the same words for the same things. That sounds obvious, but in practice language causes a surprising amount of confusion: what one organisation calls a measurement point, another calls a sensor, a location, or an observation station.

A concept scheme solves this by recording, for each concept:

  • a preferred label (prefLabel) it is known by,
  • a definition that unambiguously describes what the concept does and does not cover,
  • and optionally a scope note or example to clarify borderline cases.

Concepts rarely stand on their own. They are related hierarchically (a vehicle is broader than a truck) or associatively (a road user relates to a road segment). These relationships make it possible for queries and analyses to navigate between general and specific concepts, and to make connections between concepts explicit. Within SKOS we use broader, narrower, and related for that.

When a concept also exists elsewhere — in a national or European standard, for instance — we record that as a mapping relation. This prevents reinventing the wheel: we link to the source so interoperability with external systems remains possible.

This document describes the concept scheme as it is currently recorded. The following sections list the metadata, every concept, the relationships between them, and — where the model is connected to a data source — how it is linked to that source data.

Concepts

Traffic circulation plan

Concept details
FieldValue
DefinitionA coherent set of permanent or recurring traffic management measures designed by a road authority to guide traffic flows within an area, taking into account known and recurring traffic situations, seasonal influences and existing restrictions (e.g. school zones).
ScopeA traffic circulation plan does not itself introduce restrictions.
Sourcehttps://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_del/2022/670/oj, https://www.polisnetwork.eu/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/RTTI-Report.pdf

Policy objective

Concept details
FieldValue
DefinitionAn objective formulated at policy level - such as accessibility, liveability, road safety or air quality - that guides the desired traffic flows within a traffic circulation plan.
ScopeIn line with the POLIS/CROW position paper, policy objectives are the reason for the choice of measures and preferred routes in a traffic circulation plan.
Sourcehttps://www.polisnetwork.eu/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/RTTI-Report.pdf

Multimodal network framework

Concept details
FieldValue
DefinitionAn unambiguous representation of the multimodal use of the road network desired as a matter of policy, with a prioritisation between modes per network element.
ScopeStandardised through the CROW/LVMB guideline and manual for multimodal network frameworks. Forms the modality layer to which preferred routes and road categorisation can connect.
Sourcehttps://www.crow.nl/kennisproducten/richtlijn-en-leidraad-voor-multimodale-netwerkkaders/

Functional road classchanged

Concept details
FieldValue
Definition A classification of roads based on the importance of their functionintended use within the road network, independent of the physical road category.
ScopeBased on the INSPIRE Transport Networks FunctionalRoadClass. The POLIS/CROW position paper proposes the functional road classification as the basis (data type 2b) for the digital traffic circulation plan, with the caveat that policy purposes often require more functions than the European FRC offers and that multimodality is still lacking.
Sourcehttps://inspire.ec.europa.eu/enumeration/FunctionalRoadClassValue, https://www.polisnetwork.eu/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/RTTI-Report.pdf

FRC levels 0 to 8

Concept details
FieldValue
DefinitionThe nine levels of the functional road classification, ranging from level 0 (main road/motorway) up to level 8 (local roads of minor importance and paths).
ScopeValue list in line with the INSPIRE FunctionalRoadClassValue enumeration, as presented in the POLIS/CROW position paper. The individual level URIs follow the pattern of the INSPIRE code list register.
Sourcehttps://inspire.ec.europa.eu/enumeration/FunctionalRoadClassValue, https://www.polisnetwork.eu/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/RTTI-Report.pdf

Road category

Concept details
FieldValue
DefinitionThe physical-functional classification of a road according to the Dutch road design system, determining its layout and intended use.
ScopeBased on the Sustainable Safety (Duurzaam Veilig) categorisation (CROW). The Dutch road categories correspond functionally to the European functional road classification, but do not form a one-to-one hierarchy with it; hence a skos:related relation instead of broader/narrower.
Sourcehttps://www.crow.nl/Onderwerpen/wegontwerp-en-weginrichting/duurzaam-veilig-wegontwerp/

Flow road

Concept details
FieldValue
DefinitionA road category aimed at the through-flow of traffic at a constant, generally high speed, with grade-separated intersections.
ScopeSustainable Safety (Duurzaam Veilig) category; occurs mainly outside built-up areas.
Sourcehttps://www.crow.nl/Onderwerpen/wegontwerp-en-weginrichting/duurzaam-veilig-wegontwerp/

Distributor road

Concept details
FieldValue
DefinitionA road category that provides access to rural or urban areas: through-flow on the links, exchange at the intersections.
ScopeSustainable Safety (Duurzaam Veilig) category (CROW thesaurus). Abbreviated GOW.
Sourcehttps://data.crow.nl/thesaurus/term/8504D234-DD69-4A8D-BA4E-E207E57ECEAD, https://www.crow.nl/Onderwerpen/wegontwerp-en-weginrichting/duurzaam-veilig-wegontwerp/

Access road

Concept details
FieldValue
DefinitionA road category aimed at providing access to premises and destinations, with a residential function and a low speed.
ScopeSustainable Safety (Duurzaam Veilig) category (CROW). Abbreviated ETW.
Sourcehttps://www.crow.nl/Onderwerpen/wegontwerp-en-weginrichting/duurzaam-veilig-wegontwerp/

Residential yard

Concept details
FieldValue
DefinitionA residential area designated as a yard (Dutch: erf), bounded by signs G5 (start) and G6 (end), where pedestrians may use the full width of the road and vehicles travel at walking pace (15 km/h).
ScopeRVV 1990 (signs G5/G6). A woonerf is the most residential-oriented elaboration of an access road.
Sourcehttps://wetten.overheid.nl/BWBR0004825

Through traffic

Concept details
FieldValue
DefinitionTraffic that has neither an origin nor a destination within the area under consideration.
ScopeTerm in line with the CROW thesaurus. Keeping through traffic out of residential areas is a core objective of many traffic circulation plans.
Sourcehttps://data.crow.nl/thesaurus/term/48FA2203-699F-48A2-B82F-A0C4F6CA181C

Destination traffic

Concept details
FieldValue
DefinitionDrivers whose journey destination concerns one or more plots situated on or near a road subject to a closure declaration and reachable for that purpose solely via that road, as well as drivers of scheduled buses.
ScopeDefinition derived from art. 1 RVV 1990. Destination traffic is usually exempted from a closure declaration by means of a supplementary panel.
Sourcehttps://wetten.overheid.nl/BWBR0004825

Preferred route

Concept details
FieldValue
DefinitionA route desired by the road authority as a matter of policy, per mode or target group, intended to be shared with service providers so that navigation services can guide traffic along it.
ScopeOne of the use cases of a digital traffic circulation plan mentioned in the POLIS/CROW position paper (alongside areas to be avoided and the use of main routes).
Sourcehttps://www.polisnetwork.eu/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/RTTI-Report.pdf

Slow traffic

Concept details
FieldValue
DefinitionNon-motorised vehicles, cyclists, pedestrians and persons leading or riding an animal, as well as motor vehicles that can or may travel only at a low speed.
ScopeTerm in line with the CROW thesaurus. Relevant because circulation measures usually exempt slow traffic (e.g. a traffic filter that cyclists can still pass).
Sourcehttps://data.crow.nl/thesaurus/term/264DF64F-903B-49CD-A4E4-D3E235A20FF2

Urban vehicle access regulationchanged

Concept details
FieldValue
Definition A limitation or restriction on the use of the road network on the basis of vehicle type, traveller type or time, applying to a delimiteda regime that is valid within the defined area.
ScopeKnown by the European term UVAR (Urban Vehicle Access Regulation). Placed in DATEX II in the TrafficRegulation and ControlledZone namespaces; the NAPCORE Quality Framework for UVAR Data describes the associated data quality requirements.
Sourcehttps://datex2.eu/user-domains/uvars/, https://napcore.eu/quality/

Low emission zone

Concept details
FieldValue
DefinitionA zone from which motor vehicles that do not meet a given emission class are barred, in order to improve air quality; known across Europe as a low emission zone (LEZ).
ScopeIndicated by sign C22a, which together with the other C22 signs is being replaced by the unified sign C22e (Stb. 2025, 265).
Sourcehttps://wetten.overheid.nl/BWBR0004825, https://zoek.officielebekendmakingen.nl/stb-2025-265.html

Zero-emission zone

Concept details
FieldValue
DefinitionA zone where only zero-emission vehicles are permitted for vans and lorries.
ScopeSince 1 January 2025 municipalities have been introducing zero-emission zones for urban logistics. Indicated by sign C22c, which is being merged into the unified sign C22e (Stb. 2025, 265).
Sourcehttps://wetten.overheid.nl/BWBR0004825, https://zoek.officielebekendmakingen.nl/stb-2025-265.html

Pedestrian zone

Concept details
FieldValue
DefinitionAn area in which, in principle, only pedestrians may be present and where they have priority over other traffic.
ScopeAlso referred to as a pedestrian area; indicated by signs G7/G8 (RVV 1990).
Sourcehttps://wetten.overheid.nl/BWBR0004825

Low-car zone

Concept details
FieldValue
DefinitionAn area with deliberately limited motorised traffic, usually realised through exemptions and licence plate enforcement.
ScopeNot defined as such in the RVV; in practice a combination of closure declarations, time windows and exemptions.
Sourcehttps://wetten.overheid.nl/BWBR0004825

Time-window zone

Concept details
FieldValue
DefinitionAn area accessible to (delivery) motorised traffic only at times determined by the municipality.
ScopeTime windows are made known by means of supplementary panels accompanying a closure declaration (BABW). Widely used for urban logistics.
Sourcehttps://wetten.overheid.nl/BWBR0004826

School street

Concept details
FieldValue
DefinitionA street that is car-free during the start and end times of the school day.
ScopeMentioned as a concrete measure in the POLIS/CROW position paper; in the Netherlands usually realised through a time-bound closure declaration.
Sourcehttps://www.polisnetwork.eu/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/RTTI-Report.pdf

Limited traffic zoneremoved in 0.7.0

Concept details
FieldValue
DefinitionA delimited area within which traffic is restricted for certain vehicle categories or target groups; known internationally as ZTL (zona a traffico limitato).
ScopeGeneric zonal regime covering international UVAR practice, shaped in the Netherlands through a closure declaration with exemptions.
Sourcehttps://datex2.eu/user-domains/uvars/

One-way street

Concept details
FieldValue
DefinitionA road link where traffic is permitted in one direction of travel only.
ScopeIndicated by signs C2/C3/C4 (RVV 1990); to be modelled in DATEX II as a TrafficRegulation. One-way traffic is a widely used circulation measure.
Sourcehttps://docs.datex2.eu/levels/mastering/trafficregulation/, https://wetten.overheid.nl/BWBR0004825

Closure declaration

Concept details
FieldValue
DefinitionA prohibition on entering and using the road or road section concerned.
ScopeDefinition derived from art. 1 RVV 1990; realised through the C series of signs. Forms the legal basis for many circulation measures.
Sourcehttps://wetten.overheid.nl/BWBR0004825

No-entry restriction

Concept details
FieldValue
DefinitionA permanent access restriction for one or more vehicle categories on a specific road link.
ScopeFalls under the RTTI data category permanent access restrictions (NAPCORE data dictionary).
Sourcehttps://napcore.eu/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/DD_2022_670.pdf

Traffic filter

Concept details
FieldValue
DefinitionA physical closure - for example by means of retractable bollards - that makes through motorised traffic impossible, while cyclists, pedestrians and exemption holders can pass.
ScopeThe traffic filter is the physical realisation of a closure declaration and a core measure against rat-running traffic in traffic circulation plans.
Sourcehttps://wetten.overheid.nl/BWBR0004825

Freight delivery regulation

Concept details
FieldValue
DefinitionRegulations for the loading and unloading of goods, such as the designation of road links or areas and time-bound restrictions.
ScopeFalls under the RTTI data category freight delivery regulations (NAPCORE data dictionary); closely linked to time windows.
Sourcehttps://napcore.eu/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/DD_2022_670.pdf

Zone boundary

Concept details
FieldValue
DefinitionThe spatial delimitation of an area within which a zonal traffic rule applies.
ScopeFalls under the RTTI data category boundaries of zonal restrictions, prohibitions or obligations (NAPCORE data dictionary).
Sourcehttps://napcore.eu/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/DD_2022_670.pdf

Road linkchanged

Concept details
FieldValue
Definition A part of the road network between two consecutive positionsjunctions, serving as the smallest unit for recording road characteristics and measures.
ScopeRecorded in the Netherlands in the National Road Database (NWB) and as Wegdeel in the BGT/IMGeo. These registers serve here solely as a reference source (dct:source), not as a linking target.
Sourcehttps://docs.geostandaarden.nl/imgeo/catalogus/imgeo/, https://napcore.eu/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/DD_2022_670.pdf, https://www.nationaalgeoregister.nl/geonetwork/srv/api/records/a9b7026e-0a81-4813-93bd-ba49e6f28502

Junction

Concept details
FieldValue
DefinitionA location where traffic can be directed onto another route.
ScopeFalls under the RTTI data category junctions (NAPCORE data dictionary). For the geometric realisation, NWB, BGT/IMGeo and the Rotterdam Mercator/IBRO model serve as reference sources (dct:source).
Sourcehttps://docs.geostandaarden.nl/ibro/scope/, https://napcore.eu/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/DD_2022_670.pdf, https://www.nationaalgeoregister.nl/geonetwork/srv/api/records/a9b7026e-0a81-4813-93bd-ba49e6f28502

Introduction to RML mappingsnew in 0.7.0

An information description (technically: an RML mapping) describes how concrete data — records in a file, rows in a table, fields in an XML message — corresponds to the concepts in the concept scheme above. Without such a description data stands on its own; with it, every piece of data is connected to an unambiguous concept.

The principle is simple: for each data source we define one or more TriplesMaps. Each TriplesMap is a recipe that says "each record in this data source represents an instance of that concept, and these fields are its properties". For each field we then specify which concept describes the property (for example hasStatus, liesOnRoadSegment) and how the field should be read from the source.

The essence for the reader of this document:

  • the TriplesMap table below shows which data sources are linked to this model,
  • the subject template shows how a unique URI is constructed for each record,
  • the number of predicates gives a rough indication of how rich the mapping per source is.

The actual mapping rules (field → concept) are managed inside the Ontologica application itself and can be inspected and edited through the mapping wizard.
