This vocabularyconceptual framework describes the information needed to record where motorised traffic may travel in only one direction only. The core is the permitted direction of travel for a street: on a one-way street you may only go one wayroad, travel is allowed in a single direction only. That direction is fixed relative to the road itself: forward or return. Exceptions are common — a very frequentoften apply. A very common one is '‘except cyclists', allowing’, where cyclists to travel both ways, ormay ride in both directions. There are also exceptions for line, for example, scheduled buses or local destinationaccess-only traffic. Sometimes the one-way rulerestriction applies only at certain times (for example, for instance during rush hour or at events); this is called a dynamic one-way streetroad. Sometimes the direction of travel even changes. A reversible lane may be open in the forward direction at the start of the day and in the return direction at the end of the day. For each one-way situation, the vocabulary alsoframework records: where itthe rule applies (on which stretch of roadroad section) and in which direction, ; how road users see it (through roadsideperceive the rule: traffic signs along the road, supplementary signsplates showing exceptions or times, and advance warning signs), when it; when the rule applies (: always or only at certain times), who any exceptions, and, where the direction of travel alternates, which direction applies when; which road users are exempt; what the direction of travel is based on and, for an established direction of travel, which traffic decree applies. The framework covers every situation in which motorised traffic may travel in one direction only. For each situation, the basis of the direction of travel is recorded. In many cases, a road authority deliberately establishes the one-way restriction by traffic decree and makes the restriction known through traffic signs; exemptions and time periods may apply to. On other roads, the direction of travel follows from the traffic rules and the road layout, as on the separate carriageways of motorways and other dual carriageways, on slip roads and on roundabouts. Such an inherent direction of travel has no traffic decree and no exemptions. These situations nevertheless fall within the framework, because driving against the direction of travel can have serious consequences there, and the official decision the rule is based on. By describingsignage intended to prevent entry, such as ‘no entry’ signs at exit ramps, matters for road safety. A reversible lane always has an established direction of travel: there, the direction of travel does not follow from the traffic rules but is set by the road authority. One-way traffic is recorded because the permitted direction of travel determines which movements across the road network are possible and safe. The data support route planning per transport mode: a car or freight route follows the one-way restriction, whereas a cycle route makes use of an exemption for cyclists. The data contribute to road safety by enabling in-vehicle systems and traffic control centres to detect driving against the direction of travel and to issue warnings. For reversible lanes and dynamic one-way restrictions, the data show which direction applies at which moment. The link to the traffic decree and the signage makes it possible to verify whether the situation on the road matches the decree. Because all of this in a consistent way, different parties — such asdata is described in the same way, road authorities, navigation systems and traveltraveller information services — can reliablycan exchange and use the information. This matters especially for route planning: a cycling route needs to know a street is often usable both ways, while a car or freight route must respect the one-way ruledata reliably. The vocabulary is part offramework was drawn up within the European arrangements foragreements on travel and traffic information (the ITS Directive 2010/40/EU and the RTTI Regulation (EU) 2022/670) and connects to; the RTTI Regulation lists one-way streets and the direction of travel on reversible lanes as traffic regulations to be made available. The framework aligns with existing Dutch sources for roads and traffic signs (: NDW and the National Road DatabaseNationaal Wegenbestand (NWB, the Dutch national road database).

Introduction to semantic modelling

A concept scheme (in SKOS: ConceptScheme) is a structured collection of concepts that carry a fixed, shared meaning within a particular domain. The goal is for everyone working with the model — people and systems alike — to use the same words for the same things. That sounds obvious, but in practice language causes a surprising amount of confusion: what one organisation calls a measurement point, another calls a sensor, a location, or an observation station.

A concept scheme solves this by recording, for each concept:

  • a preferred label (prefLabel) it is known by,
  • a definition that unambiguously describes what the concept does and does not cover,
  • and optionally a scope note or example to clarify borderline cases.

Concepts rarely stand on their own. They are related hierarchically (a vehicle is broader than a truck) or associatively (a road user relates to a road segment). These relationships make it possible for queries and analyses to navigate between general and specific concepts, and to make connections between concepts explicit. Within SKOS we use broader, narrower, and related for that.

When a concept also exists elsewhere — in a national or European standard, for instance — we record that as a mapping relation. This prevents reinventing the wheel: we link to the source so interoperability with external systems remains possible.

This document describes the concept scheme as it is currently recorded. The following sections list the metadata, every concept, the relationships between them, and — where the model is connected to a data source — how it is linked to that source data.

Concepts

Basis of the direction of travelnew in 0.7.0

Concept details
FieldValue
DefinitionCharacteristic indicating what the permitted direction of travel of a one-way situation is based on: a traffic decree of the competent authority, or the traffic rules in combination with the road layout.
ScopeDetermines which other data apply. An established direction of travel requires a traffic decree and any exemptions. Where the direction of travel derives from the traffic rules and road layout, no traffic decree exists and no exemptions apply; the direction of travel is then taken from the Nationaal Wegenbestand (NWB) and the closure declaration intended to prevent entry against the direction of travel is recorded.. A reversible lane always has an established direction of travel. Not to be confused with the legal basis, which is the traffic decree itself.
ExampleA one-way street in a residential area, established by a municipal traffic decree: established by traffic decree. The right-hand carriageway of the A2 motorway: derived from traffic rules and road layout.

One-Way Traffic Data Typechanged

Concept details
FieldValue
Definition Data type within the RTTI context describing the set of concepts required to unambiguously record and exchange one-way traffic regulations on the Dutch road network. Encompasses the direction of travel, the basis of the direction of travel, the indication, any exceptions and the legal basisexemptions and, where applicable, the traffic decree.

One-Way Streetchanged

Concept details
FieldValue
Definition A road segment where traffic is allowed to movetravel in only one direction.
ScopeA one-way street rests on one of two bases (see Basis of the direction of travel). An established one-way street is set by a traffic decree and indicated by a combination of traffic signs. The core is sign C2 (one-way street, closed in this direction) on the closed side and sign C3 (one-way street) on the open side. In addition, sign C4 and mandatory direction signs (chapter D of Annex 1 to the RVV 1990), among others, can support the regulation. Supplementary signs indicate exemptions or times. The traffic signs used are described under Indication. For an inherent direction of travel, as on dual carriageways, slip roads and roundabouts, no traffic decree exists and no exemptions apply; a closure declaration intended to prevent entry against the direction of travel may be present. The one-way regulation is recorded as the direction-of-travel attribute on one or more consecutive NWB road sections; start and end follow from the boundaries of those sections.

Road Section

Concept details
FieldValue
DefinitionA section of road between two consecutive junctions or intersections, or – if no side road is present – between two points to which a traffic measure applies. In the National Road Database (NWB) a road section is the smallest unit for which road attributes are registered.

Direction of Travelchanged

Concept details
FieldValue
Definition The direction or directions in which traffic moves on a carriageway or lane. For one-way streets, this attribute unambiguously encodes the permitted direction of travelis permitted to travel along a section of road.
ScopeWithin this vocabulary, the direction of travel is the characteristic that distinguishes a one-way street from a two-way street. In the National Road Database (NWB), the direction of travel is recorded per road section, relative to the digitisation direction of that section. The attribute is essential for routing on the NWB and is also used in the NDW WKD files to record restrictions per direction. The NWB attribute does not distinguish the basis of the direction of travel: road sections with an established direction of travel and road sections with an inherent direction of travel, such as slip roads, dual carriageways and roundabouts, are coded as one-way in the same way. Both fall within this framework; the distinction is recorded with Basis of the direction of travel. Exceptions per road user category, such as ‘except cyclists’, are not included in the direction-of-travel attribute. See Exception.

Closure Declarationchanged

Concept details
FieldValue
DefinitionA closure declaration prohibiting traffic from entering a road segment in a specific direction. Indicated by sign C2 (RVV 1990): one-way street, closed in this direction for vehicles, riders and handlers of draught or pack animals or livestock.
ScopeSign C2 is the primary indication of a one-way street. The closure declaration applies to all vehicles, riders and handlers, unless an exemption is indicated by a supplementary sign. In practice, ‘except cyclists and moped riders’ is the most common exemption. Sign C2 also occurs with an inherent direction of travel, for example at the end of an exit ramp, to prevent entry against the direction of travel.

Traffic Orderchanged

Concept details
FieldValue
DefinitionA formal traffic order constituting the legal basis for imposing a one-way traffic regulation.
ScopeTraffic regulations for one-way streets currently consist largely of unstructured text. While these are published via KOOP/LVBB, a structured link to specific traffic signs and locations is not yet available as a standard feature. Only applicable in the context of basis of the direction of travel.
